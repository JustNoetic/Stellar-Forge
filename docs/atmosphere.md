# Atmosphere foundation

The renderer follows the LUT decomposition in Sébastien Hillaire's [A Scalable and Production Ready Sky and Atmosphere Rendering Technique](https://sebh.github.io/publications/egsr2020.pdf) and [reference implementation](https://github.com/sebh/UnrealEngineSkyAtmosphere). This is an independent GLSL implementation of molecular/particle media, analytic segment integration and isotropic higher-order scattering closure. Version-2 physical records add independent Mie particle populations and detached layers; see [particle optics and limits](atmosphere_particles.md).

| Pass | Size / format | Contents / invalidation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transmittance | 256 × 64, RGBA32F | RGB optical depth to atmosphere top, decoded with Beer–Lambert; radius and optical definition changes |
| Multiple scattering | 32 × 32, RGBA16F | Unit-irradiance isotropic scattering, 64 spherical directions × 24 segments, ground bounce and geometric-series closure; optical definition changes |
| Sky view | 192 × 108, two RGBA16F images | Camera-relative scattered RGB radiance and independent RGB transmittance, shared 63-interval cumulative integration; optical changes, camera altitude, source directions or irradiance |
| Aerial perspective | 192 × 108 × 64, two RGBA16F volumes plus a 192 × 108 R32F range image (20.33 MiB per retained slot) | Horizon-mapped cumulative RGB radiance and optical depth; optical changes, camera altitude, local source directions, apparent stellar sizes or irradiance |
| HDR composite | Framebuffer resolution | LUT baseline, `background * transmittance + radiance * exposure`; optional full-resolution adaptive stochastic shadow deficit with a 4/8/16-sample ceiling |

Optical LUTs are independent of the stars. Sky integration sums up to 16 scene stars using their linear RGB colors and luminosity/distance² in the same relative solar irradiance units as surface shading. Direct solar visibility fades smoothly across the reference-body horizon, using the stellar angular radius and the altitude of each integration sample. The lower integration floor remains independent of that shadow radius. Terrain-resolved solar shadowing is not modeled. Ground albedo contributes to multiple scattering, without rendering a second ground surface into the sky LUT. Zero scattering/extinction is supported without divisions by zero.

Sky directions use a radial camera basis independent of view orientation. Inside the atmosphere, the two vertical halves cover zenith→horizon and horizon→nadir with quadratic horizon concentration. Outside, they cover the projected ground disc and narrow atmospheric annulus separately. Each half has its own texel-centre range to avoid filtering across the ground/sky discontinuity. Azimuth endpoints duplicate the periodic direction. The orbital parameterization extends the usual ground sky-view mapping; it uses the same LUT integration instead of a full-resolution raymarch fallback.

`CelestialRenderer::prepare` bakes the tables before dynamic rendering; `composite_atmospheres` runs after celestial, Gaia and orbit drawing, before tone mapping. Reverse-Z depth selects sky transport for background pixels or finite-distance aerial transport for opaque pixels. Atmospheres compose far to near, and barriers cover attachments, LUT writes/reads, successive atmospheric layers, and tone mapping. With aerial perspective enabled, one cumulative column dispatch writes the aerial volumes and its final RGB radiance/transmittance directly to the sky images. The standalone 2D sky bake runs only with aerial perspective disabled. Both products retain identical angular dimensions and integration quality. GPU timers are `atmosphere_luts` (the complete preparation chain), nested `atmosphere_aerial` (the combined bake) or `atmosphere_sky` (the sky-only bake), and `atmosphere_composite`. Update counters count refreshed products, so a combined dispatch increments both sky and aerial updates.

Camera/body subtraction and the radial basis are computed in double precision before packing kilometres to float. Resources are lazy; body-owned cache slots are independent of the far-to-near composition list. All visible cache entries are reserved before assigning misses. Recently hidden entries remain resident within a 256 MiB table-payload budget, with least-recently-used inactive entries reused on pressure; stale system identities are reused first. Simultaneously visible atmospheres may exceed that budget, and allocated resources retain that high-water mark to avoid destroying resources in flight. Surface-irradiance array layers have separate stable ownership; layer reassignment rebuilds only surface irradiance. Optical cache identity retains the immutable system definition and includes generation, body index, geometry/lighting revisions, radii and coefficients. Sky and aerial cache identities add altitude and local star directions, apparent angular radii and irradiance. Their angular domain is independent of view orientation, projection and viewport resolution. Uniform buffers are separate for each frame in flight; LUT access stays ordered on the graphics queue. Rotation, FOV changes and exposure reuse both tables; translation rebuilds them when altitude or local lighting changes. Scene depth, terrain LOD and unrelated object motion are excluded from all transport cache keys. Atmospheres below half a pixel projected radius skip preparation and composition.

Atmospheric scattering currently includes stellar illumination only. Planetshine and
ringshine are applied to surfaces and rings; their atmospheric scattering paths and
experimental secondary-radiance LUTs were removed. There are no additional atmospheric
shine images, source uploads, or cache dependencies. See [secondary lighting](secondary_lighting.md).

## Finite-distance aerial perspective

The aerial LUT uses the cumulative angular-column approach inspected in KSA's `AerialPerspectiveLut.comp` and `AtmosphereLuts.glsl`. Its XY domain shares the sky-view mapping: azimuth and horizon-focused zenith angle, with separate ground/sky halves and a compressed orbital limb. Camera rotation and FOV changes therefore do not move the integration grid.

Each XY invocation intersects its ray with the atmosphere and lower floor, stores the integration range, and marches progressively through 63 intervals. Slice zero explicitly stores zero radiance and zero optical depth. Slice distances are squared fractions of the column length inside the atmosphere and linear fractions outside it. Two midpoint cells per interval use the existing hydrostatic/particle medium evaluator, detached-layer subdivisions, per-star source terms, transmittance LUT and isotropic multiple-scattering LUT. Both angular horizon boundary rows carry their ground/sky branch explicitly when clipping the integration segment. Sky and aerial bakes use the same integration helper and distance intervals, so their complete columns agree.

At composition, full-resolution reverse-Z depth supplies the finite endpoint. Vacuum before atmosphere entry is excluded. The endpoint's atmospheric distance is normalized by the analytic column range; near the angular horizon or within 10 m of the lower floor, the stored range avoids unstable geometric reconstruction. Depth interpolation uses physical distances between slices, including the first interval, so short paths converge to identity. Angular reconstruction uses four hardware-filtered samples per volume for bicubic B-spline filtering; vertical footprints are clamped to their own horizon half and azimuth wraps periodically. RGB transmission is decoded from the interpolated optical depth. There is no frustum-neighbor padding, slant rescaling, or difference of two scattering-table values.

At each integration sample, host solar visibility is `smoothstep(-w, w, mu - mu_horizon)`, with `w = shadow_radius / sample_radius * stellar_angular_radius`. The per-star angular radius is derived from its physical radius and host distance. This is an approximate solar-disc transition rather than an exact disc integral. Multiple scattering remains available after direct sunset. Generic optical/surface-irradiance tables retain a fixed 0.01-radian reference source because those tables are shared across stars. External body/ring deficits retain full-resolution endpoints with optional guarded software VRS and use the same per-star direct visibility as the clear aerial source.

`aerial_perspective_enabled` defaults to true. Disabling it suppresses inscattering on opaque and transparent surfaces and skips aerial updates; RGB extinction still applies at each surface endpoint through the optical-depth LUT and short-path midpoint limit. Sky pixels retain full transport. Re-enabling reuses a still-valid volume or rebuilds it after altitude/lighting/optical changes. Objects beyond a complete atmospheric column reuse sky radiance and transmission, so a distant ring cannot introduce a full-column brightness seam. The volume has no temporal history and never reads scene depth, terrain LOD, or external caster state.

Curved surface depth must preserve the reference curvature: ordinary rasterized triangle chords lie below the intended surface and can expose sphere tessellation or quadtree geomorph patches as denser air. The shared sphere/terrain fragment shader uses `common/surface_depth.glsl` to correct depth by intersecting the view ray with an ellipsoid whose radius preserves interpolated relief after removing reference-curvature chord sag. Only near-side fragments receive the near intersection; far-side terrain retains its rasterized depth so camera-facing slopes beyond the horizon cannot move in front of their occluders. Terrain vertices carry a separate base chord through the same parent interpolation and morph as their positions; this does not flatten real negative relief. Coverage remains rasterized geometry, so silhouette antialiasing remains limited by geometry LOD and single-sample rendering. The correction uses fragment depth writes and is shared by the normal, cached-FP64 and reference terrain paths.

## State and editing

`BodyDefinition::atmosphere` defaults to disabled when no atmosphere data is supplied. Complete pressure, temperature and composition records automatically enable physical atmospheres, including original-format system data; an explicit disabled setting is preserved. Physical inputs use familiar pressure and temperature units in the inspector; density, height and scattering are calculated automatically. The inspector edits pressure, temperature and gas percentages, and shows calculated properties; raw optical parameters remain available in JSON. Manual optical mode remains available; physical mode derives molecular optics and altitude profiles from pressure, temperature, gas composition and body gravity. See `docs/atmosphere_physics.md`.

`ApplyBodyAtmosphere` validates the whole draft and rejects stale system/body revisions. Successful changes publish a new immutable definition and increment definition/lighting revisions; dynamics and terrain geometry remain valid. Apply changes the running simulation; Save atmosphere to system writes the applied atmosphere into that body's stored system definition. JSON serialization and N-body snapshot export preserve optical parameters. Missing or incomplete atmosphere data loads disabled; complete pressure/temperature/composition records automatically enable physical mode unless explicitly disabled. System load and body creation also validate atmosphere definitions. `show_atmospheres` is the global visibility switch.

## Terrain floor and altitude reference

`BodyDefinition::height_range_km` parses and persists the established terrain minimum/maximum from `system.json`; missing ranges default to `[0, 0]`. Reversed/nonfinite ranges or minima through the body centre are rejected. Both Earth presets declare `[-10.984, 8.848]` km. The lower integration bound is the minimum of zero, the established minimum, compatible active imported elevation metadata, and the enabled procedural displacement envelope. No terrain payload reads or geographic searches are added to the atmosphere path.

The transport domain is a sphere in homothetically flattened atmosphere coordinates, aligned with the snapshot pole. This maps altitude zero onto the reference ellipsoid instead of treating the poles as kilometres below an equatorial spherical surface. The lower sphere radius is `reference_radius + minimum_height / polar_ratio`, conservatively enclosing signed terrain displacement along ellipsoid normals. The outer radius remains `reference_radius + height_km`, independent of the lower floor. This is still the spherical LUT transport approximation: optical distance and vertical scales use atmosphere coordinates, with shape error of order oblateness relative to physical kilometres (about 0.34% for Earth). Exact oblate radiative transfer remains future work.

Molecular density uses `exp(-h / H_R)` with **signed height from the reference ellipsoid**, independently of the LUT floor. Lowering the floor preserves sea-level scattering coefficients and adds denser molecular air at negative altitude. The aerosol profile uses `exp(-max(h, 0) / H_M)`, a constant continuation below zero rather than exponentially multiplying the short aerosol scale height into an opaque underground layer. This describes manual optical mode. Physical mode instead samples a simulation-prepared hydrostatic density profile; both modes keep explicit aerosol profiles.

The camera clamps only to the lower floor, not to sea level. Rays integrate to the analytic lower ellipsoid or atmosphere exit. Scene depth is never read while building either the sky LUT or the aerial volume. Opaque pixels receive transport only up to their full-resolution reconstructed distance.

An earlier attempt projected scene depth into the sky LUT, filtered its ray endpoints, and rejected those endpoints against full-resolution depth. This produced patchy surface haze and stippling at depth/terrain discontinuities, and made sky caches depend on camera rotation. That path and its endpoint rejection have been removed. The image-wide black-surface preservation regression remains active with aerial perspective disabled: extinction must never add light to black geometry.


## Current limits and extension points

This pass supplies sky, orbital limbs and finite-distance aerial perspective, including opaque objects behind an atmospheric column. Rings and orbit lines retain per-fragment depth in the shared transparency A-buffer; atmospheric transport transforms their premultiplied radiance with RGB extinction and alpha-weighted inscattering before per-pixel depth resolution. See [transparency.md](transparency.md) for ordering, dual-source composition, memory bounds and overflow behavior. The analytic lower ellipsoid approximates host solar shadowing and ground bounce; terrain-resolved sunlight visibility is not yet modeled. There is no water medium yet: the below-zero domain supports air-filled depressions and exposed negative terrain, not atmospheric air physically filling an ocean.

Physical atmospheres couple direct sunlight attenuation and hemispherical diffuse sky irradiance into sphere/terrain lighting. Manual optical mode retains its previous surface lighting. Transparent rings and orbit lines receive transport only to their own endpoints. Overlapping atmospheres compose as separate columns rather than a mixed medium. Physical mode adds validated gas composition, hydrostatic density profiles, mixed-gas and ozone absorption, and cached surface sunlight/skylight. Shared graphics/compute composition preserves RGB transmittance; dual-source graphics blending is device-feature gated with a compute fallback. External bodies and rings shadow atmospheric scattering through an adaptive stochastic deficit with guarded software VRS and single-frame reconstruction, including suppression of cached multiple-scattering fill inside shadows; see [atmospheric shadows](atmosphere_shadows.md) for shared mesh penumbrae, bounds, verification and cost. Full finite solar-disc transport, eclipse-aware multiple scattering, refraction, clouds and weather remain outside the model. See `docs/atmosphere_physics.md` for model bounds, cache ownership, surface irradiance and verification.

## Verification

`StellarRenderingTests --atmosphere-cache-only` checks distance-order swaps, temporary and complete invisibility, stable per-body surface layers, insertion ahead of warmed entries, optical-edit invalidation, and inactive LRU eviction under a twenty-body workload. Reordering and retained visibility changes must produce zero optical and surface-irradiance rebuilds; a newly visible body builds its own optical and surface tables; camera-dependent angular tables may refresh after position changes. This test also runs in `--atmospheres-only`.

`StellarRenderingTests --atmosphere-columns-only` compares the combined bake's sky radiance and RGB throughput against the standalone sky shader across all texels in 28 vacuum, molecular/particle, detached-aerosol and giant-radius ground/orbit cases. Both outputs must match bit for bit. This comparison also runs in `--atmospheres-only`, whose aerial contracts check unchanged toggle reuse, sky-only updates while disabled, and a combined refresh after movement while disabled.

The [combined-column validation and timing report](atmosphere_column_merge.md) records exact output parity and approximately 49% lower LUT preparation time during camera translation on the RTX 4060 Ti, with the same resolution and integration quality.

A direct GPU solar-visibility regression checks full daylight/night, partial visibility when the central solar ray intersects the host, and half visibility at the local horizon for three altitudes and three stellar angular radii. FOV changes and an asymmetric-lighting azimuth seam are checked without rebuilding the angular LUT.

Horizon regressions evaluate the shared surface depth correction on near/far fragments for spherical and oblate hosts, scan finite scattering across the ground/sky LUT boundary at 10 m, 1 km and 12 km altitudes, and compare grazing RGB extinction to independent 8192-step numerical integration on both sides of that boundary.

`StellarStateTests` covers command validation, stale edits, immutable snapshots, revision dependencies, default-disabled compatibility, definition serialization and N-body export. `StellarRenderingTests --atmospheres-only` covers orbital radiance/limb bounds, ground sky color, analytic zenith RGB transmittance above and below zero, sea-level normalization, bounded aerosol continuation, polar altitude, image-wide sky-only opaque surface preservation at orbit/ground horizons and small rotations, finite-distance RGB extinction against analytic and numerical references, orbital vacuum entry, short-path identity, curved-surface invariance across sphere/quadtree LOD/geomorphs, opaque-limb profile against an independent single-scattering reference, horizon-filtered background preservation, host shadow, foreground depth, multiple atmospheric bodies, multi-star linearity, exposure, vacuum, cache reuse, translated world coordinates, and horizon-column contracts (analytic extinction across altitudes/distances, rotation/FOV cache reuse, translation refresh, optical invalidation, background isolation, and monotonic continuous terminator transitions). `--atmosphere-preview scratch/atmosphere-preview` exports synthetic sky/limb images and a resolved terminator diagnostic. `--atmosphere-surface-preview scratch/atmosphere-surface-preview` settles the local Earth color/elevation assets and exports atmosphere-on/off orbital and horizon PPM images (horizon-mapped aerial perspective) for visual inspection. Set `STELLAR_VULKAN_VALIDATION=1` to request validation layers. CTest registers `atmosphere_rendering_contracts` when GPU tests are enabled.

`StellarRenderingTests --atmosphere-benchmark scratch/atmosphere-floor/comparison.json` compares zero and -10.984 km floors at 1920x1080. It measures cached frames, camera translation, camera rotation, and complete optical updates in ground-horizon/orbit views with three alternating-order repetitions (8 warmup + 24 measured draws per case). GPU timers exclude readback/presentation; this is not application FPS. LUT dimensions, memory and integration sample counts are identical for both floors. The report separates aerial baking, the full preparation chain, composition, and opaque surface drawing (including curvature-corrected depth). Report cold/update timings separately from cached composition; driver clock ramping can dominate such short GPU intervals.

The lower-floor comparison uses the same table dimensions and sample counts in both modes. Camera rotation reuses both sky and aerial tables.

On the RTX 4060 Ti at 1920×1080, the padded 64×36×64 implementation measured median aerial updates of 0.026–0.044 ms and composition of 0.080–0.177 ms across the tested ground-horizon/orbit moving views. Total LUT preparation plus composition was 0.12–0.24 ms for camera rotation/translation. These serialized fixture measurements exclude opaque drawing, readback and presentation; they are not application frame times or a hardware-independent guarantee.

Horizon-column measurements on the RTX 4060 Ti at 1920×1080: median aerial rebuilds were 0.105–0.116 ms, complete sky+aerial preparation during translation was 0.222–0.243 ms, and composition was 0.110–0.241 ms across the synthetic orbit/ground fixtures and both floor settings. Camera rotation performed no LUT rebuilds. These serialized offscreen measurements exclude opaque drawing, readback and presentation; they are not application frame times. The 192×108 angular domain is more expensive to refresh than the former 64×36 frustum grid. The benchmark report for this migration is `scratch/aerial-after/benchmark.json`.
