# Atmosphere foundation

The renderer follows the LUT decomposition in Sébastien Hillaire's [A Scalable and Production Ready Sky and Atmosphere Rendering Technique](https://sebh.github.io/publications/egsr2020.pdf) and [reference implementation](https://github.com/sebh/UnrealEngineSkyAtmosphere). This is an independent GLSL implementation of molecular/particle media, analytic segment integration and isotropic higher-order scattering closure. Version-2 physical records add independent Mie particle populations and detached layers; see [particle optics and limits](atmosphere_particles.md).

| Pass | Size / format | Contents / invalidation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transmittance | 256 × 64, RGBA32F | RGB optical depth to atmosphere top, decoded with Beer–Lambert; radius and optical definition changes |
| Multiple scattering | 32 × 32, RGBA16F | Unit-irradiance isotropic scattering, 64 spherical directions × 24 segments, ground bounce and geometric-series closure; optical definition changes |
| Sky view | 192 × 108, two RGBA16F images | Camera-relative scattered RGB radiance and independent RGB transmittance, 48 segments; optical changes, camera altitude, source directions or irradiance |
| Aerial perspective (Frustum Volume) | 64 × 36 × 64, two RGBA16F volumes (2.25 MiB per retained atmosphere slot) | Prefix scattered RGB radiance and RGB optical depth; camera position/orientation/projection and optical/lighting changes |
| Aerial perspective (Bruneton 4D) | 256 × 128 × 32, two RGBA16F 3D textures (8 MiB per retained atmosphere slot) | Precomputed single Rayleigh + multiple scattering and single Mie tables parameterized by $(\nu, \mu_s, \mu, r)$; optical definition changes only (view- and camera-independent) |
| HDR composite | Framebuffer resolution | LUT samples only, `background * transmittance + radiance * exposure`; no per-pixel ray integration |

Optical LUTs are independent of the stars. Sky integration sums up to 16 scene stars using their linear RGB colors and luminosity/distance² in the same relative solar irradiance units as surface shading. An analytic inner ellipsoid blocks direct sunlight; it is a conservative terrain floor rather than terrain-resolved solar shadowing. Ground albedo contributes to multiple scattering, without rendering a second ground surface into the sky LUT. Zero scattering/extinction is supported without divisions by zero.

Sky directions use a radial camera basis independent of view orientation. Inside the atmosphere, the two vertical halves cover zenith→horizon and horizon→nadir with quadratic horizon concentration. Outside, they cover the projected ground disc and narrow atmospheric annulus separately. Each half has its own texel-centre range to avoid filtering across the ground/sky discontinuity. Azimuth endpoints duplicate the periodic direction. The orbital parameterization extends the usual ground sky-view mapping; it uses the same LUT integration instead of a full-resolution raymarch fallback.

`CelestialRenderer::prepare` bakes the tables before dynamic rendering; `composite_atmospheres` runs after celestial, Gaia and orbit drawing, before tone mapping. Reverse-Z depth selects sky transport for background pixels or finite-distance aerial transport for opaque pixels. Atmospheres compose far to near, and barriers cover attachments, LUT writes/reads, successive atmospheric layers, and tone mapping. GPU timers are `atmosphere_luts` (the complete preparation chain), nested `atmosphere_sky` and `atmosphere_aerial`, and `atmosphere_composite`.

Camera/body subtraction and the radial basis are computed in double precision before packing kilometres to float. Resources are lazy and retained to the high-water mark of simultaneously resolved atmospheric bodies, avoiding destruction of resources in flight. Optical cache identity retains the immutable system definition and includes generation, body index, geometry/lighting revisions, radii and coefficients. Sky cache identity adds altitude and local star directions/irradiance. Frustum aerial cache identity additionally includes the view/projection and atmosphere basis, whereas Bruneton 4D tables depend solely on optical parameters and are completely invariant to camera rotation and translation. Uniform buffers are separate for each frame in flight; LUT access stays ordered on the graphics queue. Rotation rebuilds only the aerial volume when using the frustum model, and rebuilds neither table under Bruneton; exposure rebuilds neither table. Scene depth, terrain LOD and unrelated object motion are excluded from all transport cache keys. Atmospheres below half a pixel projected radius skip preparation and composition.

## Finite-distance aerial perspective

Two selectable models are supported, controlled by `RenderSettings::aerial_perspective_bruneton` and exposed in the inspector UI ("Bruneton (Precomputed 4D)" vs "Frustum Volume"):

### Bruneton 4D precomputed scattering (default)

The Bruneton model parameterizes single scattering and isotropic multiple scattering over a 4D domain $(r, \mu, \mu_s, \nu)$ packed into two 256×128×32 3D textures (`bruneton_rayleigh` and `bruneton_mie`), where the $X$ axis packs $\nu$ (8 bins) and $\mu_s$ (32 bins). Precomputation evaluates 48 integration steps with smooth solar disc visibility (`sun_visibility`), which smoothly attenuates direct sunlight across the solar disc angular radius against the reference ellipsoid rather than cutting off at the $-11$ km floor.

In composite, each screen pixel evaluates its exact view ray $(x, d)$ and reconstructed opaque endpoint $p$:
1. **Transmittance**: Evaluated as $\exp(-\max(\tau_x-\tau_p,0))$ from precomputed optical depths in the 2D transmittance LUT, reversing the ray for ground-intersecting columns. This is the transmittance ratio in log space: long grazing columns can have unrepresentably small transmittances while their finite segment still transmits light. Storing optical depth in FP32 avoids half-float underflow, denominator clamping, and cancellation from quantized optical depths. For short paths ($< 2.0$ km), it blends smoothly with analytic midpoint exponential integration (`smoothstep(0.5, 2.0, path)`).
2. **Inscattering**: Evaluated as the difference $L(x, d) - T \cdot L(p, d)$ from the 4D scattering textures, folded with Mie phase $\mathcal{P}_M(\nu)$ and per-star irradiance, blended with short-path direct integration.

This model directly solves the three artifacts of the frustum volume:
- **Terminator stair-stepping**: Eliminated by 48-step quadrature in the 4D LUT and smooth solar-disc visibility.
- **Entry altitude banding**: Eliminated because radial distance is mapped continuously via texture coordinate $r$ rather than snapped to moving distance slices.
- **Mountain below horizon bleeding**: Eliminated because every pixel evaluates its exact endpoint independently without reading neighbor voxels or padding across silhouettes.
- **Performance**: Zero rebake cost during camera translation or rotation (view-independent planet-centric parameterization).

### Frustum volume

One compute invocation per frustum XY column integrates progressively through 64 distance slices, with two midpoint quadrature samples per interval. It reuses the transmittance and multiple-scattering tables and the same medium/source model as the sky. Two volumes retain scattered radiance and colored optical depth separately; composition uses `surface * exp(-tau) + radiance * exposure`. Volume generation never reads scene depth. Full-resolution reverse-Z reconstruction supplies the opaque endpoint in atmosphere-space kilometres, analytically excludes vacuum before entry, and clamps transport to the atmospheric segment.

Distance slices follow a signed hyperbolic-sine distribution around the dense part of the analytic column. At orbit, the focus approaches the conservative floor intersection or tangent point; near ground it approaches the camera to retain short-path resolution. The mapping covers at least the longest atmospheric tangent chord, independently of vacuum distance. Slice positions relative to this focus are shared by all columns, preventing integration-bin phase changes from imprinting a grid on orbital surfaces. The full-resolution sampler interpolates the four surrounding columns at their own mapped depths and uses physical distance between slice endpoints for depth interpolation, including the first interval crossing atmosphere entry. For orbital views, neighboring rays are sampled at the same endpoint radius. Missing angular samples are padded with the nearest supported column, with a bounded two-column guard search if the bilinear footprint has no support. This extrapolates transport past the silhouette without allocating another volume. The full-resolution opaque-depth mask clips the padded result; background and sky-limb pixels remain bitwise identical to sky-only rendering. Near grazing angles, a geometric density-shell estimate adjusts optical path length and rescales radiance through the corresponding Beer–Lambert factor. This is an approximate angular reconstruction with an effective Rayleigh/Mie scale height, not additional ray integration. The alignment fades out near ground and over the first kilometre so short paths converge to identity. If an atmosphere is too small for any neighboring column to represent the endpoint, the sky column supplies a fractional-column approximation. This remains a low-resolution angular approximation, especially near silhouettes and sharp forward-scattering lobes.

`aerial_perspective_enabled` defaults to true and is exposed beside the global atmosphere switch. Disabling it restores sky-only composition and bitwise preservation of opaque pixels; it also suppresses aerial updates. Re-enabling rebuilds a stale volume. Paused views reuse both sky and aerial tables. The initial implementation has no temporal history or additional terrain, ring, or body shadow sampling.

Curved surface depth must preserve the reference curvature: ordinary rasterized triangle chords lie below the intended surface and can expose sphere tessellation or quadtree geomorph patches as denser air. The shared sphere/terrain fragment shader uses `common/surface_depth.glsl` to correct depth by intersecting the view ray with an ellipsoid whose radius preserves interpolated relief after removing reference-curvature chord sag. Only near-side fragments receive the near intersection; far-side terrain retains its rasterized depth so camera-facing slopes beyond the horizon cannot move in front of their occluders. Terrain vertices carry a separate base chord through the same parent interpolation and morph as their positions; this does not flatten real negative relief. Coverage remains rasterized geometry, so silhouette antialiasing remains limited by geometry LOD and single-sample rendering. The correction uses fragment depth writes and is shared by the normal, cached-FP64 and reference terrain paths.

Bruneton scattering precomputation carries the ground/sky branch explicitly into coordinate decoding. The last ground row and first sky row share the same normalized boundary coordinate but represent different rays; inferring the branch from that coordinate misdecodes the ground horizon row.

## State and editing

`BodyDefinition::atmosphere` defaults to disabled when no atmosphere data is supplied. Complete pressure, temperature and composition records automatically enable physical atmospheres, including original-format system data; an explicit disabled setting is preserved. Physical inputs use familiar pressure and temperature units in the inspector; density, height and scattering are calculated automatically. The inspector edits pressure, temperature and gas percentages, and shows calculated properties; raw optical parameters remain available in JSON. Manual optical mode remains available; physical mode derives molecular optics and altitude profiles from pressure, temperature, gas composition and body gravity. See `docs/atmosphere_physics.md`.

`ApplyBodyAtmosphere` validates the whole draft and rejects stale system/body revisions. Successful changes publish a new immutable definition and increment definition/lighting revisions; dynamics and terrain geometry remain valid. Apply changes the running simulation; Save atmosphere to system writes the applied atmosphere into that body's stored system definition. JSON serialization and N-body snapshot export preserve optical parameters. Missing or incomplete atmosphere data loads disabled; complete pressure/temperature/composition records automatically enable physical mode unless explicitly disabled. System load and body creation also validate atmosphere definitions. `show_atmospheres` is the global visibility switch.

## Terrain floor and altitude reference

`BodyDefinition::height_range_km` parses and persists the established terrain minimum/maximum from `system.json`; missing ranges default to `[0, 0]`. Reversed/nonfinite ranges or minima through the body centre are rejected. Both Earth presets declare `[-10.984, 8.848]` km. The lower integration bound is the minimum of zero, the established minimum, compatible active imported elevation metadata, and the enabled procedural displacement envelope. No terrain payload reads or geographic searches are added to the atmosphere path.

The transport domain is a sphere in homothetically flattened atmosphere coordinates, aligned with the snapshot pole. This maps altitude zero onto the reference ellipsoid instead of treating the poles as kilometres below an equatorial spherical surface. The lower sphere radius is `reference_radius + minimum_height / polar_ratio`, conservatively enclosing signed terrain displacement along ellipsoid normals. The outer radius remains `reference_radius + height_km`, independent of the lower floor. This is still the spherical LUT transport approximation: optical distance and vertical scales use atmosphere coordinates, with shape error of order oblateness relative to physical kilometres (about 0.34% for Earth). Exact oblate radiative transfer remains future work.

Molecular density uses `exp(-h / H_R)` with **signed height from the reference ellipsoid**, independently of the LUT floor. Lowering the floor preserves sea-level scattering coefficients and adds denser molecular air at negative altitude. The aerosol profile uses `exp(-max(h, 0) / H_M)`, a constant continuation below zero rather than exponentially multiplying the short aerosol scale height into an opaque underground layer. This describes manual optical mode. Physical mode instead samples a simulation-prepared hydrostatic density profile; both modes keep explicit aerosol profiles.

The camera clamps only to the lower floor, not to sea level. Rays integrate to the analytic lower ellipsoid or atmosphere exit. Scene depth is never read while building either the sky LUT or the aerial volume. Opaque pixels receive transport only up to their full-resolution reconstructed distance.

An earlier attempt projected scene depth into the sky LUT, filtered its ray endpoints, and rejected those endpoints against full-resolution depth. This produced patchy surface haze and stippling at depth/terrain discontinuities, and made sky caches depend on camera rotation. That path and its endpoint rejection have been removed. The image-wide opaque-surface preservation regression remains active with aerial perspective disabled.


## Current limits and extension points

This pass supplies sky, orbital limbs and finite-distance aerial perspective, including opaque objects behind an atmospheric column. Rings and orbit lines retain per-fragment depth in the shared transparency A-buffer; atmospheric transport transforms their premultiplied radiance with RGB extinction and alpha-weighted inscattering before per-pixel depth resolution. See [transparency.md](transparency.md) for ordering, dual-source composition, memory bounds and overflow behavior. The analytic lower ellipsoid approximates host solar shadowing and ground bounce; terrain-resolved sunlight visibility is not yet modeled. There is no water medium yet: the below-zero domain supports air-filled depressions and exposed negative terrain, not atmospheric air physically filling an ocean.

Physical atmospheres couple direct sunlight attenuation and hemispherical diffuse sky irradiance into sphere/terrain lighting. Manual optical mode retains its previous surface lighting. Transparent rings and orbit lines receive transport only to their own endpoints. Overlapping atmospheres compose as separate columns rather than a mixed medium. Physical mode adds validated gas composition, hydrostatic density profiles, mixed-gas and ozone absorption, and cached surface sunlight/skylight. Shared graphics/compute composition preserves RGB transmittance; dual-source graphics blending is device-feature gated with a compute fallback. There is no volumetric shadowing by other bodies/rings, full finite solar-disc integration, refraction, clouds or weather. See `docs/atmosphere_physics.md` for model bounds, cache ownership, surface irradiance and verification.

## Verification

Horizon regressions evaluate the shared surface depth correction on near/far fragments for spherical and oblate hosts, scan finite scattering across the ground/sky LUT boundary at 10 m, 1 km and 12 km altitudes, and compare grazing RGB extinction to independent 8192-step numerical integration on both sides of that boundary.

`StellarStateTests` covers command validation, stale edits, immutable snapshots, revision dependencies, default-disabled compatibility, definition serialization and N-body export. `StellarRenderingTests --atmospheres-only` covers orbital radiance/limb bounds, ground sky color, analytic zenith RGB transmittance above and below zero, sea-level normalization, bounded aerosol continuation, polar altitude, image-wide sky-only opaque surface preservation at orbit/ground horizons and small rotations, finite-distance RGB extinction against analytic and numerical references, orbital vacuum entry, short-path identity, curved-surface invariance across sphere/quadtree LOD/geomorphs, opaque-limb profile against an independent single-scattering reference, padded-sampler background preservation, host shadow, foreground depth, multiple atmospheric bodies, multi-star linearity, exposure, vacuum, cache reuse, translated world coordinates, and the complete Bruneton 4D contract suite (analytic extinction across altitudes/distances, rotation/translation cache invariance, optical-only rebake, background isolation, and monotonic continuous terminator transitions). `--atmosphere-preview scratch/atmosphere-preview` exports synthetic sky/limb images. `--atmosphere-surface-preview scratch/atmosphere-surface-preview` settles the local Earth color/elevation assets and exports atmosphere-on/off orbital and horizon PPM images (both frustum and Bruneton models) for visual inspection. Set `STELLAR_VULKAN_VALIDATION=1` to request validation layers. CTest registers `atmosphere_rendering_contracts` when GPU tests are enabled.

`StellarRenderingTests --atmosphere-benchmark scratch/atmosphere-floor/comparison.json` compares zero and -10.984 km floors at 1920x1080. It measures cached frames, camera translation, camera rotation, and complete optical updates in ground-horizon/orbit views with three alternating-order repetitions (8 warmup + 24 measured draws per case). GPU timers exclude readback/presentation; this is not application FPS. LUT dimensions, memory and integration sample counts are identical for both floors. The report separates aerial baking, the full preparation chain, composition, and opaque surface drawing (including curvature-corrected depth). Report cold/update timings separately from cached composition; driver clock ramping can dominate such short GPU intervals.

The lower-floor comparison uses the same table dimensions and sample counts in both modes. Camera rotation now reuses sky tables, so the depth-aware rotation refresh cost of the previous implementation no longer applies.

On the RTX 4060 Ti at 1920×1080, the padded 64×36×64 implementation measured median aerial updates of 0.026–0.044 ms and composition of 0.080–0.177 ms across the tested ground-horizon/orbit moving views. Total LUT preparation plus composition was 0.12–0.24 ms for camera rotation/translation. These serialized fixture measurements exclude opaque drawing, readback and presentation; they are not application frame times or a hardware-independent guarantee.
