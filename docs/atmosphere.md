# Atmosphere foundation

The renderer follows the LUT decomposition in Sébastien Hillaire's [A Scalable and Production Ready Sky and Atmosphere Rendering Technique](https://sebh.github.io/publications/egsr2020.pdf) and [reference implementation](https://github.com/sebh/UnrealEngineSkyAtmosphere). This is an independent GLSL implementation of exponential Rayleigh/Mie media, analytic segment integration, and isotropic higher-order scattering closure.

| Pass | Size / format | Contents / invalidation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transmittance | 256 × 64, RGBA16F | RGB Beer–Lambert transmittance to atmosphere top; radius and optical definition changes |
| Multiple scattering | 32 × 32, RGBA16F | Unit-irradiance isotropic scattering, 64 spherical directions × 24 segments, ground bounce and geometric-series closure; optical definition changes |
| Sky view | 192 × 108, two RGBA16F images | Camera-relative scattered RGB radiance and independent RGB transmittance, 48 segments; optical changes, camera altitude, source directions or irradiance |
| HDR composite | Framebuffer resolution | LUT samples only, `background * transmittance + radiance * exposure`; no per-pixel ray integration |

Optical LUTs are independent of the stars. Sky integration sums up to 16 scene stars using their linear RGB colors and luminosity/distance² in the same relative solar irradiance units as surface shading. An analytic inner ellipsoid blocks direct sunlight; it is a conservative terrain floor rather than terrain-resolved solar shadowing. Ground albedo contributes to multiple scattering, without rendering a second ground surface into the sky LUT. Zero scattering/extinction is supported without divisions by zero.

Sky directions use a radial camera basis independent of view orientation. Inside the atmosphere, the two vertical halves cover zenith→horizon and horizon→nadir with quadratic horizon concentration. Outside, they cover the projected ground disc and narrow atmospheric annulus separately. Each half has its own texel-centre range to avoid filtering across the ground/sky discontinuity. Azimuth endpoints duplicate the periodic direction. The orbital parameterization extends the usual ground sky-view mapping; it uses the same LUT integration instead of a full-resolution raymarch fallback.

`CelestialRenderer::prepare` bakes optical and sky-view tables before dynamic rendering; `composite_atmospheres` runs after celestial, Gaia and orbit drawing, before tone mapping. The compute composite uses reverse-Z depth as an opaque/background mask and modifies only background pixels in the HDR storage image. Atmospheres compose far to near, and barriers cover attachments, LUT writes/reads, successive atmospheric layers, and tone mapping. GPU timers are `atmosphere_luts` (the complete LUT preparation chain), nested `atmosphere_sky`, and `atmosphere_composite`.

Camera/body subtraction and the radial basis are computed in double precision before packing kilometres to float. Resources are lazy and retained to the high-water mark of simultaneously resolved atmospheric bodies, avoiding destruction of resources in flight. Optical cache identity retains the immutable system definition and includes generation, body index, geometry/lighting revisions, radii and coefficients. Sky cache identity adds altitude and local star directions/irradiance. Uniform buffers are separate for each frame in flight; LUT access stays ordered on the graphics queue. Camera rotation and exposure do not rebuild LUTs. Sky tables depend on camera altitude and local source lighting; view/projection matrices, scene depth, terrain LOD and unrelated object motion are excluded from sky cache keys. Atmospheres below half a pixel projected radius skip preparation and composition.

## State and editing

`BodyDefinition::atmosphere` defaults to disabled. Earth explicitly enables the default optical preset in both stored system definitions. Other bodies retain their appearance until enabled. The inspector's Atmosphere tab exposes height and scale heights in kilometres, RGB scattering/extinction in km⁻¹, Mie asymmetry, and ground albedo. The Earth optical preset can be applied to any planetary body. These are editable optical inputs, not a chemical composition model.

`ApplyBodyAtmosphere` validates the whole draft and rejects stale system/body revisions. Successful changes publish a new immutable definition and increment definition/lighting revisions; dynamics and terrain geometry remain valid. Apply changes the running simulation; Save atmosphere to system writes the applied atmosphere into that body's stored system definition. JSON serialization and N-body snapshot export preserve optical parameters. Missing atmosphere data loads disabled. System load and body creation also validate atmosphere definitions. `show_atmospheres` is the global visibility switch.

## Terrain floor and altitude reference

`BodyDefinition::height_range_km` parses and persists the established terrain minimum/maximum from `system.json`; missing ranges default to `[0, 0]`. Reversed/nonfinite ranges or minima through the body centre are rejected. Both Earth presets declare `[-10.984, 8.848]` km. The lower integration bound is the minimum of zero, the established minimum, compatible active imported elevation metadata, and the enabled procedural displacement envelope. No terrain payload reads or geographic searches are added to the atmosphere path.

The transport domain is a sphere in homothetically flattened atmosphere coordinates, aligned with the snapshot pole. This maps altitude zero onto the reference ellipsoid instead of treating the poles as kilometres below an equatorial spherical surface. The lower sphere radius is `reference_radius + minimum_height / polar_ratio`, conservatively enclosing signed terrain displacement along ellipsoid normals. The outer radius remains `reference_radius + height_km`, independent of the lower floor. This is still the spherical LUT transport approximation: optical distance and vertical scales use atmosphere coordinates, with shape error of order oblateness relative to physical kilometres (about 0.34% for Earth). Exact oblate radiative transfer remains future work.

Molecular density uses `exp(-h / H_R)` with **signed height from the reference ellipsoid**, independently of the LUT floor. Lowering the floor preserves sea-level scattering coefficients and adds denser molecular air at negative altitude. The aerosol profile uses `exp(-max(h, 0) / H_M)`, a constant continuation below zero rather than exponentially multiplying the short aerosol scale height into an opaque underground layer. This remains an optical density model, not a hydrostatic pressure/temperature/composition solver.

The camera clamps only to the lower floor, not to sea level. Sky rays integrate to the analytic lower ellipsoid or atmosphere exit. Scene depth is never read while building the sky LUT. The full-resolution composite skips every opaque pixel before evaluating atmospheric transport; terrain and opaque objects therefore remain bit-for-bit unchanged. This deliberately leaves finite-distance surface haze for a separate aerial-perspective implementation.

An earlier attempt projected scene depth into the sky LUT, filtered its ray endpoints, and rejected those endpoints against full-resolution depth. This produced patchy surface haze and stippling at depth/terrain discontinuities, and made sky caches depend on camera rotation. That path and its endpoint rejection have been removed. The image-wide opaque-surface preservation regression fails on that implementation and passes on the sky/background-only composite.


## Current limits and extension points

This pass supplies sky and orbital limbs. Surface aerial perspective, including attenuation of opaque stellar discs seen through another body's atmosphere, remains deferred. Every opaque depth pixel is excluded rather than receiving an approximate full sky column. Transparent objects do not supply depth for finite-distance ordering. The analytic lower ellipsoid approximates host solar shadowing and ground bounce; terrain-resolved sunlight visibility is not yet modeled. There is no water medium yet: the below-zero domain supports air-filled depressions and exposed negative terrain, not atmospheric air physically filling an ocean.

Surface incident sunlight still uses the existing surface renderer; its atmospheric attenuation and diffuse skylight are not yet coupled. Transparent rings do not supply depth for partial-column ordering. Overlapping atmospheres compose as separate columns rather than a mixed medium. There is no volumetric shadowing by other bodies/rings, finite solar-disc integration, chemical composition, ozone/Chappuis absorption, refraction, clouds, weather, or dual-source graphics blending. The compute composite already preserves colored transmittance; future blend paths must preserve it too. Absorption profiles belong in the shared medium evaluator, shadow visibility in the sky integrator, and composition-derived coefficients in simulation-owned definitions.

## Verification

`StellarStateTests` covers command validation, stale edits, immutable snapshots, revision dependencies, default-disabled compatibility, definition serialization and N-body export. `StellarRenderingTests --atmospheres-only` covers orbital radiance/limb bounds, ground sky color, analytic zenith RGB transmittance above and below zero, sea-level normalization, bounded aerosol continuation, polar altitude, image-wide opaque surface preservation at orbit/ground horizons and small rotations, host shadow, foreground depth, multiple atmospheric bodies, multi-star linearity, exposure, vacuum, cache reuse, and translated world coordinates. `--atmosphere-preview scratch/atmosphere-preview` exports synthetic sky/limb images. `--atmosphere-surface-preview scratch/atmosphere-surface-preview` settles the local Earth color/elevation assets and exports atmosphere-on/off orbital and horizon PPM images for visual inspection. Set `STELLAR_VULKAN_VALIDATION=1` to request validation layers. CTest registers `atmosphere_rendering_contracts` when GPU tests are enabled.

`StellarRenderingTests --atmosphere-benchmark scratch/atmosphere-floor/comparison.json` compares zero and -10.984 km floors at 1920x1080. It measures cached frames, sky updates, and complete optical updates in ground/orbit views with three alternating-order repetitions (8 warmup + 24 measured draws per case). GPU timers exclude readback/presentation; this is not application FPS. LUT dimensions, memory and integration sample counts are identical for both floors. Report cold/update timings separately from cached composition; driver clock ramping can dominate such short GPU intervals.

The lower-floor comparison uses the same table dimensions and sample counts in both modes. Camera rotation now reuses sky tables, so the depth-aware rotation refresh cost of the previous implementation no longer applies.
